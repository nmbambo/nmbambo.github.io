I The problem

Many decisions cite evidence that does not bear the weight placed on it, and AI estimates now arrive with a confidence they have not earned. The gap is rarely dishonesty; it is untested inference.

II Approach

  1. State each decision as a claim with the evidence offered for it, and rate that evidence on a ladder from anecdote to tested causal finding.
  2. Use tabular in-context-learning models (TabDPT-Turbo and TabICLv2) for fast, distributional estimates on modest data, while pinning context rows, order and decoding so every estimate replays.
  3. Hold the causal boundary: these models are correlational, so an estimate may never be bound to a decision variable without causal support.
  4. Treat external models such as Jev as proposals only, fed masked or synthetic data, and record their influence on the decision explicitly.
  5. Score process quality at decision time and outcome quality after; good process with a bad outcome updates the forecast, bad process with a good outcome raises a flag.

III Structure

  1. ClaimWhat the decision asserts
  2. EvidenceWhat is offered, rated on a ladder
  3. EstimateTabular ICL distributions, pinned and replayable
  4. TestThe cheapest falsifying check
  5. LearningOutcome scored apart from process
