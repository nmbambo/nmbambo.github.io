Executive summary

A South African lender wants its small-business credit model to decline weak applications without a credit officer signing each one. The record shows why the answer is yes, but not for everyone: the model and the officers agree 94% of the time overall but only 71% for applicants with thin credit files, and "default" means 90 days past due to the model but 60 days to Collections. The decision automates declines where the evidence supports it, keeps a human look where it does not, meets POPIA s71 by design, and carries a stop rule that reverts the change the same day if it misbehaves.

Reading time: about eight minutes. Each section below is one field of the record.

1. The question

Should the Lender let its small-business credit model decline applications of up to R250,000 that score below the approved cut-off, without a credit officer reviewing each one?

Why now. A decline takes a median six working days. Officers spend about a quarter of their time confirming declines the model already predicted.

Reversibility. Costly to reverse: applicants declined in error do not come back.

Deliberately out of scope. Changing the cut-off (a risk-appetite decision for the Credit Risk Committee), automating approvals, and applications above R250,000.

2. Authority

Owner Head of SME Credit
Mandate Credit Policy s4.2: delegated authority to change operating procedures within risk appetite, granted by the Credit Risk Committee before this decision
Cannot Change risk appetite, the cut-off or the model; waive data-subject or consumer-credit obligations
Sign-offs Model Risk Manager (validated for unattended use, with conditions) · Information Officer (POPIA s71, with conditions) · Head of Compliance (decline wording)
Escalates to Credit Risk Committee

The point of this section: a record that only says who decided proves that someone decided. This one shows they were entitled to, and where their entitlement stops.

3. Meaning: the terms the decision turns on

Term Used by the model Used elsewhere Effect on the decision
default 90+ days past due within 12 months Collections: 60+ days. Finance: IFRS 9 Stage 3 Bad rate is 7.8% on one definition and 11.2% on the other. If applicants rank differently at 60 days, the cut-off declines the wrong people
decline Refused, no counter-offer Officers also record "approved at a lower amount" as a decline The 31% officer decline rate overstates refusals, so the back-test benchmark is partly counter-offers
thin-file applicant Fewer than two bureau trade lines and under 12 months of statements — Defines who keeps a human review

This is where most automated decisions quietly go wrong. Nobody disagreed about the model. They disagreed about the word.

4. Evidence, by kind

Claim Kind Caveat
E1 14,200 applications; 31% declined by officers observed Includes counter-offers
E2 Median decline turnaround 6 working days observed —
E3 Model Gini 0.52 on the 2023–24 holdout inferred Validated as decision support, not unattended use
E4 Officers agreed with the model on 94% of below-cut-off cases; 71% for thin-file inferred Measures agreement with officers, not correctness
E5 "Competitors already auto-decline" asserted Unverified; given no weight

The tag matters more than the number. E4 is the strongest evidence for automation and it is circular: it treats officers' decisions as the truth. The record says so, and the review (section 9) is designed to break the circle.

5. Assumptions that would flip the decision

Assumption Flips it? Test
A1 2023–24 applicants represent today's applicants Yes Population stability of recent scores against the holdout
A2 Outside thin-file, officers add little the model lacks Yes Reversal rate on representations during the pilot
A3 Applicants will use a representation channel if offered clearly No Monthly take-up

6. Constraints, classified

Constraint Kind Binding Who could relax it
K1 POPIA s71: solely automated decisions with legal effect need appropriate measures, including a chance to make representations and information about the logic legal yes nobody
K2 A refused applicant may ask for the dominant reason (National Credit Act) legal yes nobody
K3 "Every decline is signed by an officer" habit no Head of SME Credit: it is in the procedure manual, not the policy
K4 Twelve officers, no new headcount economic yes CFO

K3 is the interesting one. It felt like a rule. It was an earlier decision nobody had revisited.

7. Options

Option For Against
O1 Keep officer review of every decline Familiar Keeps a habit at a measurable cost to applicants
O2 Automate every decline below the cut-off Fastest; frees ~25% of officer time 71% thin-file agreement; representation under K1 becomes nominal
O3 Automate below the cut-off, except thin-file applicants; every automated decline carries its reason and an invitation to make representations Same-day for most; frees ~20%; meets K1 and K2 by design Representations may absorb the time released
O4 Defer until "default" is reconciled and the model re-validated Cleaner basis Four months' delay with nothing learned

8. The decision

Chosen: O3. Decided by the Head of SME Credit on 29 September 2026.

Rationale. The evidence supports automation where officers and the model already agree and does not support it for thin-file applicants. O3 automates only the first group. The "default" reconciliation runs as a condition with a stop rule rather than a precondition, because it can halt the pilot if it changes the ranking.

Rejected. O1: keeps a habit, not a requirement. O2: thin-file agreement too low to remove the human look. O4: the reconciliation is needed but does not need to block.

Dissent. The Head of Collections argued that the model understates risk because it was trained on 90 days. Response: accepted as a live risk; condition C4 re-scores the cohort on 60 days within 60 days, and the pilot pauses if the ranking changes.

Predicted outcome, stated before execution.

  • Automated declines answered in under one working day (from six).
  • Officer time on decline confirmation down at least 15%.
  • Fewer than 10% of representations reverse the decline.
  • No rise in decline complaints.

9. Execution conditions

Condition Type Owner If breached
C1 Thin-file routing tested on 500 historical applications, zero misroutes precondition Head of SME Operations Do not start
C2 Decline letters give the dominant reason and how to make representations precondition Head of Compliance Do not start
C3 Representations reviewed within 5 working days ongoing Head of SME Credit Two weeks over SLA: all declines return to officers
C4 Cohort re-scored on the 60-day definition within 60 days ongoing Model Risk Manager Pilot pauses until reported
C5 Stop rule: reversal rate over 15% in any month, 60-day Gini below 0.45, or any province or sector's decline rate moving more than 5 points stop rule Head of SME Credit Revert to O1 the same day; report to the Committee

10. Review

Reviewer: Chief Risk Officer, not the decider. By: 30 April 2027, or sooner if a stop rule fires, a complaint reaches a regulator or ombud, or the model or cut-off changes.

Criterion Measure Threshold
R1 Applicants got faster answers Median days to decline, automated only Under 1
R2 The automated declines were the right ones Reversals on representation; 12-month bureau performance of declined applicants who borrowed elsewhere Reversals under 10%; their arrears at least twice the Lender's book
R3 No group was disadvantaged Decline rate by province and formal/informal sector against baseline No move over 5 points
R4 Capacity was released, not just moved Officer hours on confirmations plus representations At least 15% below baseline

R2 is what breaks the circularity in E4: it judges the declines against what later happened to the applicants, not against what officers would have done.

At review, the record asks one more question: was this a good decision with a bad outcome, a bad decision with a good outcome, or neither? The decision is judged on what was knowable on 29 September 2026, which is why everything above was written down then.

Why this matters

Decisions like this one are being made every week in South African lenders, insurers and public bodies as models move from advising to acting. Most are recorded as a committee minute and a model validation report. Neither shows the meaning gap in "default", the habit hiding in K3, or the stop rule that makes the change safe to try.
