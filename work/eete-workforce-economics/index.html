· What it gives you

If you want workforce decisions you can defend to a board, a regulator or a union,

The full method applied to one domain: costs, scenarios and trade-offs laid out with their evidence, and the decision left with you.

I The problem

Employment is one of the largest economic agreements most people and firms make, yet it is priced and judged with little transparency on either side.

II Approach

  1. Treat the employee as a service provider and the employment contract as a service agreement with measurable terms.
  2. Analyse the economics of a role from both sides: what it costs, what it returns and how fairly it is priced.
  3. Start with concierge-style pilots for recruiters and enterprise HR and rewards teams before building the full platform.
  4. Turn each analysis into a decision a recruiter, employer or employee can explain and defend.

III Structure

  1. InputsRole, contract and market data
  2. AnalysisCost, value and fairness of the employment agreement
  3. DecisionA defensible recommendation with its evidence
  4. PlatformCareer economics decision intelligence, after the pilots

· What the engine does

EETE takes the details of a job offer and a candidate's own situation and works out what the offer really costs and really pays: after tax, after commute, after every mandatory cost the job imposes on daily life. It produces a plain signal, shows the numbers and what would change them, and stops there. It is not financial, tax, legal or employment advice.

Gross-to-net position

What the offer pays before tax, and what actually lands after PAYE, UIF and retirement and medical deductions.

Mandatory cost load

Costs this specific job imposes: commute, a required wardrobe, self-supplied technology, location-driven housing. Never generic lifestyle spending.

Effective hourly rate

Net pay divided by the real hours the job takes, commute included. Equal salaries can differ sharply here.

The signal

Accept, caution or reject: a plain read of whether the numbers clear the published thresholds, the same for every candidate, every time.

Cash surplus

Money in minus money out: the number a bank balance would show.

Economic surplus

One step further: it also counts the value of the person's own time. A job can be cash-positive every month and still economically negative once you count how much of a life it takes.

· One calculation, five readers

Every report comes from the same underlying calculation, rendered for the person reading it. Nobody gets a different number; some readers get less detail.

ReportForWhat it shows
Candidate ReportThe candidateTheir own economics, resilience, role fit and relocation impact, never the employer's internal assessment of them
Offer Defensibility ReportThe recruiter presenting the offerThe full hiring picture: cost of acceptance, churn risk, market context and business justification, alongside the candidate's economics

Every report carries a decision-reasons section drawn only from figures already on the page. If a recruiter or employer decides differently from the signal, the report says so and records the reason they gave, so nobody reads a decision without knowing whether a person overrode the numbers. Each report also carries a right of reply.

· Six standing positions

What the platform does and doesn't do, held in the code rather than in a policy document.

No protected attributes

Race, gender, disability, marital status, religion, health and credit or criminal-record data don't exist in the domain model. That's structural isolation, not a filter applied late.

A human always decides

The single autonomous mechanism only routes a report to a faster or more thorough review. It never issues a decision, and human overrides are disclosed on the report.

Issued records are immutable

Every report carries a cryptographic fingerprint anyone can recompute. A changed input produces a new report linked to the one it supersedes, never a silent edit.

No bare numbers

Every point estimate carries a range, robustness or sensitivity read. No module infers beyond structured input, and each states what it doesn't invent.

Personal information protected

The scanner sees only what it is explicitly given. Pilots run under a written operator agreement under POPIA, with purpose-limited processing and deletion on request.

Real deletion

A delete actually deletes. Only a minimal audit row with no personal information may remain, and nothing is presented as deleted when it isn't.

· Built as a data product

The engine's real asset is its contract: a typed calculation request in, a typed result out, exported as versioned JSON Schema. Integrations are built against the schema, never against source code, and input and output hashes give every result its lineage.

  1. Model the input, optional with a sensible default, so existing requests keep validating.
  2. Model the output, with a plain statement of what it computed from and what it deliberately did not invent.
  3. Write a pure function that never fabricates a value; a missing signal is reported as missing.
  4. Wire it into the pipeline before the result is hashed.
  5. Bump the engine version, minor for anything additive.
  6. Test every load-bearing branch, not just that it runs.
  7. Expose it in the console if a reader needs to see it.
  8. Document it wherever it touches personal information or compliance.
  9. Verify it for real: tests, lint and schema export, then a live click-through.
