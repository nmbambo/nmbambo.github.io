I Who this is for

The Decision Maturity Assessment is designed for governance, risk, data, transformation and enterprise architecture leaders who need to understand whether their organisation can account for its important decisions, not merely describe what it intended to achieve.

It is particularly relevant when you are preparing for an audit, investigating a failed initiative, strengthening decision governance or making AI-assisted decisions more defensible.

You do not need to begin with a full transformation programme. You need a clear view of your current capability and the gaps that matter most.

II The problem it diagnoses

Strategic intent, evidence, authority and execution can drift apart.

Leadership may know what an organisation intended to do without being able to reconstruct how an important decision was reached, what justified it, who had authority to make it, or whether execution remained faithful to that decision.

This accumulated gap is decision debt. The assessment examines five fundamental questions:

  1. FramingWas the decision clearly defined, with its objectives, constraints and relevant context established?
  2. EvidenceWere the available evidence, assumptions, uncertainties and alternatives recorded adequately?
  3. JudgementWas the decision made by the appropriate authority, with a defensible rationale?
  4. ExecutionCan the organisation establish what was implemented and whether it remained consistent with the decision?
  5. ReviewCan the organisation evaluate the result, attribute outcomes appropriately and learn from the evidence?

The objective is to identify where decision integrity is supported by evidence, where it is fragile, and what should be addressed first. The assessment evaluates the organisation’s decision-making capability; it does not claim that any individual decision was right or that outcomes can be predicted.

III What you receive

The assessment produces a concise, inspectable deliverable grounded in the evidence reviewed.

  1. A scored decision maturity map covering framing, evidence, judgement, execution and review.
  2. An evidence-based gap analysis identifying the most consequential weaknesses and explaining the reasoning behind the findings.
  3. Prioritised recommendations indicating practical interventions, their intended purpose and the dependencies that may affect implementation.
  4. An executive summary that communicates the findings clearly to decision-makers without requiring them to master the underlying methodology.

Where useful and agreed, the assessment can also include an anonymised or appropriately sanitised decision-record example using your organisation’s terminology.

Scores are accompanied by their supporting rationale and material evidence limitations. The assessment distinguishes demonstrated capability from assumptions, incomplete evidence and areas requiring further investigation.

IV Scope, inputs, duration and success

Inputs required

  • Four to six recent or recurring high-stakes decisions, selected to give a useful view of the decision cycle.
  • Access to approximately three to six relevant decision owners, participants or governance stakeholders.
  • Available documentation on decision authority, evidence, procedures, execution and review.

Duration

  • Typically two to four calendar weeks, depending on access to stakeholders, documentation and the agreed scope.
  • Limited interviews, document review and structured analysis. No software installation is required.

The assessment can identify evidence gaps where documentation is incomplete. Missing evidence is recorded as a limitation, not automatically treated as proof of failure.

How success is judged. The assessment should leave your organisation with a shared, evidence-based understanding of where decision integrity is strong, where it is fragile, and which practical interventions deserve priority. The findings should be clear enough for leadership to decide what to address, what additional evidence is needed and whether deeper investigation is justified.

V What happens next

The assessment stands alone. It is intended to provide value independently. You are not required to commission further work.

If the findings reveal material gaps, possible next engagements include:

  1. Decision Audit BlueprintA targeted investigation of specific decisions, evidence trails, authority structures or execution failures.
  2. Decision Canvas & Governance StandardA consistent approach to decision framing, documentation, accountability and review.
  3. Meaning & Coherence EngineeringAddressing deeper inconsistencies between organisational meaning, data, processes and decision rules.

Any follow-on engagement is separately scoped against the findings and your priorities.
