· What it gives you

If you want to know whether an offer will hold before anyone signs it,

A verdict a remuneration committee can interrogate line by line: the real cost of the role, the full cost of acceptance, the churn risk with its drivers, and the decision left with you.

I The problem

An employment offer is one of the most consequential decisions an organisation makes and one of the least documented. It is judged from the employer's side only, so offers that look affordable on paper fail in month four.

II Approach

  1. Calculate the cost of living the role actually requires: housing, commute, debt service, schooling, clothing and technology, line by line.
  2. Set out the full cost of acceptance: recruitment, onboarding, training, first-year cost to company and time to full productivity.
  3. Estimate twelve-month churn risk from seniority, economic margin, skills fit and experience band, each driver quantified with its range, and stress-test the result.
  4. Give a verdict a remuneration committee can interrogate line by line: justified, justified with conditions, or caution. The hiring decision stays with the employer.

III Structure

  1. Role fitOverlap against supplied skill lists; no black-box matching
  2. Market positionPercentile against declared market data
  3. Cost of acceptanceThe full economic weight of the hire
  4. Churn riskDrivers, weights and ranges, labelled as a heuristic
  5. Audit trailEach stage marked explicit or inferred
