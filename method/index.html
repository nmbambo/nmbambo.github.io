The Method

The method is the product. Everything built on the Spine follows one documented way of deciding.

Most decision work stops at description: a dashboard, a report, an opinion. This method carries an organisation further, from enabling a single decision, to making its decisions cohere, to executing them and learning from what actually happened.

I The position

Decision enablement is the first step, not the destination. Each stage depends on the one before it, and the last returns to the first as correction.

Ingqiqo works as a nudger, in the tradition of libertarian paternalism: it shapes how choices are presented so better decisions are easier, while leaving the choice with the people who own it. Its buyers are the choice architects inside organisations.

II Three commitments

These are what let a small firm deliver the kind of clarity that used to arrive only in a boardroom binder.

Method, not headcount

The work follows one documented method, so it does not depend on large teams of consultants. Anyone can trace an output back to the same steps, sources and assumptions.

Instruments, not one-off reports

Every offering is a repeatable instrument. It can be run again next quarter, and the results compared, instead of ageing on a shelf.

Working shown, not hidden

Every figure traces to its source, its assumptions and its range. A client can check it, challenge it and reuse it.

III The Disciplined Decision Cycle

IDDC, the Ingqiqo Disciplined Decision Cycle, turns evidence and business meaning into decisions that can be checked, contested and repeated. It begins from an established data-modelling discipline and extends past it into execution and outcome learning.

  1. Discover. Find the decision, the people who hold it, and the evidence that exists.
  2. Connect. Tie the evidence to shared meaning: terms, entities and relationships.
  3. Review. Test the evidence and the assumptions against a clear standard.
  4. Facilitate. Bring the decision-holders together on the same picture.
  5. Compare. Lay out the alternatives, and what each one gives up.
  6. Consolidate. Settle on one decision, recorded with its evidence and authority.
  7. Publish. Release it as a governed record that others can rely on and contest.
  8. Execute. Carry it out within declared bounds, watching for deviation.
  9. Learn. Score the outcome separately from the process, and feed the difference back.

The cycle sits where business architecture, knowledge engineering, decision intelligence, operations research and AI governance meet. Its constitution sets out roles, evidence standards, an epistemic ladder, decision contracts, solver policies, contestation and outcome learning, and it grows by addition rather than by removing what came before.

IV Three rules

These hold in every engagement, at every size.

  1. One run, many viewsEach engagement produces one canonical result. Every stakeholder gets a view shaped for their role, but nobody gets a different number.
  2. Every figure has a pedigreeEach number is labelled: observed, declared by someone, taken from a published table, or estimated by a model. Estimates carry ranges, so it is always clear how much weight a figure can bear.
  3. People decideThe instruments calculate, compare and explain. They name the decision required and who holds the authority to make it, and stop there.

V How decisions are understood

Not every decision is a calculation. The method distinguishes how a decision is produced before it judges whether it was good.

Rational

Deliberate weighing of options against stated objectives and constraints: the territory of optimisation and the solver.

Behavioural

Choices shaped by framing, reference points and bias. The method debiases openly; it never nudges a principal toward its own recommendation.

Nature-driven

Outcomes produced by thresholds, selection and emergence rather than by anyone choosing. These are named as such, so nobody is blamed for a decision nobody made.

A second axis runs across all three: ordinary, tradeable values versus protected values. Protected values are treated as hard limits on what may be chosen, never as heavy weights that a large enough gain could buy out.
