I How it works

  1. Choose one important decision your organisation made in the last year, and name it.
  2. For each of fifteen criteria, say what happened for that decision and what you could actually show for it. A record from the decision counts fully; a rule that requires it counts up to 2; what people would say counts up to 1. This is the rubric used in the full assessment.
  3. If you do not know, say so. An unknown is recorded as a limitation, not as a failure.
  4. You receive a report with a certificate ID that anyone can check at nmbambo.github.io/verify. Run it again later and the new version shows what changed in your answers and what each change implies.

A self-check is your own account of one decision. It is not the Decision Maturity Assessment, in which the evidence is examined across four to six decisions.

II The decision

A few words you will recognise later, for example "2026 credit limit policy change". It appears on your certificate, so leave out anything confidential.

VIII Your details

Your report is issued to you and stored by Ingqiqo Executables so it can be verified and rerun. Your name and email do not appear on the certificate; your organisation and the decision name do.

Consent under POPIA

Responsible party: Ingqiqo Executables (Pty) Ltd, South Africa. Data is held with Supabase in the EU (Frankfurt). To see, correct or erase your data, write to nmbambo@ingqiqo-executables.run. Erased certificates show as withdrawn on the verify page.

Want the evidence examined, not reported?

The Decision Maturity Assessment examines four to six decisions against their records, over two to four weeks, and tells you where decision integrity holds and where it depends on memory.
