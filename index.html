Meaning, made coherent.

I The cost no one counts

Every organisation pays for its decisions twice: once when they are made, and again, quietly, when they are made badly, made twice, or never finished.

Meaning drifts

Finance, operations and the board each hold a different number for the same thing. Meetings go on deciding which figure is right instead of what to do.

Clarity is rationed

Rigorous decision work has long arrived in a boardroom binder, at a consulting firm’s price. Most decisions never get it.

Accountability blurs

When an outcome goes wrong, nobody can show who decided, on what evidence, under what authority. Blame lands where it is easiest, not where it belongs.

My work makes that clarity ordinary: one method, instruments that run again, and the working shown, so any decision can be checked, contested and owned.

II Practice

One posture, five demands. These are not five separate roles. The Solutionist posture holds them together: one practitioner who carries all of them, and calls on each as the effort demands it. You get one accountable person across the whole problem, not five hand-offs.

Scientist

Frames the question before the answer. Tests whether evidence actually supports a decision, and says so when it does not.

Engineer

Builds the pipelines and platforms that carry meaning intact, from raw data to governed, auditable gold.

  • Solution Engineer (Agile) builds the solution increment by increment.
  • Solution Train / Release Train Engineer keeps the trains of teams delivering in step.
  • Delivery Engineer gets it into production and keeps it running.

Analyst

Reads what the numbers are saying, and what they are hiding, then turns it into a choice someone can make.

  • Business Analyst on needs, processes and requirements.
  • Systems Analyst on how systems behave and connect.
  • Quantitative Analyst on the numbers and the models.
  • Subject Matter Analyst on the depth of the domain.
  • Visual Designer makes the findings legible at a glance.

Modeller

Gives terms, taxonomies and ontologies their shape, and models decisions so they can be represented, compared and reviewed.

Architect

Designs the whole: how data, process and decision fit together, and where accountability sits within them.

  • Solution Architect shapes the solution end to end.
  • Delivery Architect designs how it is delivered and handed over.

Human in the loop, by design

The machines calculate. People do the thinking.

Every piece of this work keeps the cognitive part of the job with people: framing the question, weighing what matters, choosing, and answering for the choice. AI and automation are used hard, for speed and reach, but only ever to propose. That is the difference you are buying.

Stays with people

  • Framing. What the decision really is, and whose it is.
  • Judgement. What counts, what is protected, what trade-off is acceptable.
  • Deciding. The choice is made and signed by a named person with the authority.
  • Accountability. Who answers for it, and when it is reviewed.

Handed to machines

  • Calculating. Costs, scenarios, ranges and constraints, at speed.
  • Retrieving. The evidence, with its source and its pedigree.
  • Proposing. Options and estimates, each marked as a proposal.
  • Recording. What was proposed, what was accepted, and by whom.

No model sets a weight, asserts a constraint or issues a decision. Every AI contribution is logged as a proposal a person accepted or rejected, so you get the speed of AI without handing it your judgement, and an audit trail that will stand up to a board or a regulator.
